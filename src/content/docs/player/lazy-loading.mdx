`lazy` (`data-lazy="true"`) defers a player's expensive work until it comes
within **200px of the viewport**. Every lazy player on the page shares one
IntersectionObserver.

```html
<div data-waveform-player data-lazy="true" data-url="/audio/track.mp3" data-waveform="/audio/track.json"></div>
```

```js
new WaveformPlayer('#player', { url: '/audio/track.mp3', lazy: true });
```

## What waits, and what doesn't

The player itself is built at once: it is registered in
`WaveformPlayer.instances`, its methods work, and `WaveformPlayer.init()` treats
it as initialised. Until it nears the viewport it shows what it shows while
loading — the title, the loading state and any known [`duration`](/player/options/#source) —
at its **full height**, so nothing shifts when it loads.

What waits:

- assigning the audio source (so not even a metadata request goes out),
- fetching a `.json` peaks sidecar,
- decoding the audio for peaks,
- drawing the waveform.

Anything that needs the audio loads it straight away: `play()` (and so
`togglePlay()`, the play button and Space), a seek, `load()` or `loadTrack()`.
`autoplay` waits for the player to be loaded. In external mode `lazy` defers the
peaks the same way. Where IntersectionObserver is missing, the player loads as
if it weren't lazy.

Measured in Chromium against the 2.0 core: a page of 30 lazy players loaded
the 2 in view, then all 30 after scrolling through.

## Lazy or zero-bandwidth?

| | `lazy` | [`preload: 'none'` + `duration` + inline peaks](/player/zero-bandwidth/) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audio requests before play | none until in view, then a metadata request (with the default `preload`) | none |
| Peaks | fetched or decoded when in view | inline, drawn at once |
| Best for | players whose peaks are `.json` files or must be decoded | catalogues you can generate peaks for |

They combine: `lazy` on a zero-bandwidth player also postpones drawing until
it's in view.

## In the frameworks and extensions

- **Astro**: `<WaveformPlayer lazy>` now sets the core's `lazy` option
  (`data-lazy`). In 1.x it was the component's own IntersectionObserver
  (`data-waveform-player-lazy`), which is gone — see
  [Migrating from 1.x](/getting-started/migrating-from-1x/#astro-lazy).
- **React, Vue, Svelte**: a `lazy` prop on the player component.
- **WaveformPlaylist** never passes `lazy` to its player (it would only defer
  the playlist's own single player). The playlist's Astro component keeps its
  own `lazy` prop, which defers building the whole playlist.
- **WaveformSounds**: `lazy` has no effect on its engine, which loads nothing
  until a sound plays anyway.

<Aside type="note">
`lazy` is not `loading="lazy"` for audio: a lazy player that is played before it
scrolls into view simply loads then.
</Aside>
